| Document                                         | J.Crew Blocker                                               | Serta Blocker                                                | Chewy Blocker                                                | LME Risk Assessment                                          |
| :----------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Aurora - Credit Agreement.pdf**                | Yes. "none of Holdings, the Borrower or any Restricted Subsidiary shall be permitted to transfer (including, without limitation, pursuant to Investments (including through the designation of a Restricted Subsidiary as an Unrestricted Subsidiary), disposition, asset sales, Restricted Payments or otherwise), or grant an exclusive license in, any Material Intellectual Property to any Unrestricted Subsidiary"[^1] | Yes. The agreement may not be amended to "subordinate (A) the Liens on all or any material portion of the Collateral securing the Secured Obligations (“Existing Liens”) to the Liens securing any other Indebtedness or (y) any Secured Obligations in contractual right of payment to any other Indebtedness"[^2] without the consent of each adversely affected Lender. | No. A Subsidiary Loan Party is automatically released from its obligations under the Loan Documents if it is designated an Unrestricted Subsidiary.[^3] | **Medium Risk.** While the agreement has strong J.Crew and Serta blockers, the absence of a Chewy blocker presents a risk. A valuable subsidiary could be designated as unrestricted and then be used to raise debt that is structurally senior to the existing debt, without the existing lenders having a say. |
| **Evergreen - Credit Agreement.pdf**             | No. The agreement contains a representation that "none of the IP Rights owned by any Credit Party or any of its Subsidiaries is subject to any licensing agreement"[^4], but this is not a negative covenant and there are no restrictions on transferring IP to an unrestricted subsidiary. | Yes. The agreement cannot be amended to "subordinate the Obligations to any other Indebtedness without the written consent of each Lender directly and adversely affected thereby"[^5]. | No. A guarantor is released from its obligations if it ceases to be a subsidiary or becomes an Excluded Subsidiary.[^6] | **High Risk.** The lack of a J.Crew blocker means that the borrower could transfer valuable intellectual property to an unrestricted subsidiary, which could then be used to raise debt outside the credit group. The absence of a Chewy blocker adds to the risk. |
| **Flexera (Thoma Bravo) - Credit Agreement.pdf** | Yes. "no Subsidiary may be designated as an Unrestricted Subsidiary if it owns or exclusively licenses any Material Intellectual Property at the time of designation"[^7]. | No. There is no explicit Serta blocker in the amendments section. The agreement can be amended to subordinate the obligations with the consent of the Required Lenders, not each affected lender. | Yes. A subsidiary guarantor is not released from its guarantee if it "continues to be a borrower or guarantor in respect of any Indebtedness constituting a Junior Financing"[^8]. | **Low Risk.** The presence of both J.Crew and Chewy blockers provides strong protection against asset leakage and debt subordination by moving assets to unrestricted subsidiaries. The lack of a Serta blocker is a minor weakness, but the other protections mitigate this risk. |
